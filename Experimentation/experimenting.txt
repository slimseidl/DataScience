Types of Studies 

    - Experiments 

        - an experiment is defined by comparing outcomes between two or more groups and ensuring equivalence between the compared groups except for the manipulation that we want to test
        - see if a change in one feature has an effect in the value of a second feature
            -  seeing if changing the layout of a button on a website causes more visitors to click on it

    - Observational Studies

        - no control is exerted on the variables of interest, perhaps due to ethical concerns or a lack of power
        - look at the effects of smoking on health, the potential risks make it unethical to force people into smoking

    - Quasi-Experiments

        - some, but not all, of the control requirements of a true experiment, are met
        - rolling out a new website interface to all users to see how much time they spend on it
        - manipulation is controlled by the experimenter, there aren't multiple groups to compare


Descriptive and Inferential Statistics 

    - Descriptive statistics is about describing our collected data using measures of center, spread, shape, Distribution, and outliers 
    - Inferential Statistics is about using our collected data to draw conclusions to a larger population
    - Performing inferential statistics well requires that we take a sample that accurately represents our population of interest
        - Population - our entire group of interest.
        - Parameter - numeric summary about a population
        - Sample - a subset of the population
        - Statistic - numeric summary about a sample

    Sampling Distribution

        - Take random samples of data 
        - Plot all possible combinations on a histogram 
        - All of these are the sampling Distribution

Law of Large Numbers 

    - the larger our sample size, the closer our statistic gets to the parameter
    - If estimating the mean:
        - if sample size is 500 out of 5,000,000, going to be further away from actual mean 
        - if sample is 500,000 out of 5million, will be closer to actual mean 
    
Central Limit Theorum 

    - with a large enough sample size the sampling distribution of the mean will be normally distributed
    - Applies to: 
        - Sample means 
        - Sample proportions
        - Difference in sample means (xbar1 - xbar2)
        - Difference in sample proportions
