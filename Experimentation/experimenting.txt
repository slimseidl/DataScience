Types of Studies 

    - Experiments 

        - an experiment is defined by comparing outcomes between two or more groups and ensuring equivalence between the compared groups except for the manipulation that we want to test
        - see if a change in one feature has an effect in the value of a second feature
            -  seeing if changing the layout of a button on a website causes more visitors to click on it

    - Observational Studies

        - no control is exerted on the variables of interest, perhaps due to ethical concerns or a lack of power
        - look at the effects of smoking on health, the potential risks make it unethical to force people into smoking

    - Quasi-Experiments

        - some, but not all, of the control requirements of a true experiment, are met
        - rolling out a new website interface to all users to see how much time they spend on it
        - manipulation is controlled by the experimenter, there aren't multiple groups to compare


Descriptive and Inferential Statistics 

    - Descriptive statistics is about describing our collected data using measures of center, spread, shape, Distribution, and outliers 
    - Inferential Statistics is about using our collected data to draw conclusions to a larger population
    - Performing inferential statistics well requires that we take a sample that accurately represents our population of interest
        - Population - our entire group of interest.
        - Parameter - numeric summary about a population
        - Sample - a subset of the population
        - Statistic - numeric summary about a sample

    Sampling Distribution

Law of Large Numbers 

    - the larger our sample size, the closer our statistic gets to the parameter
    - If estimating the mean:
        - if sample size is 500 out of 5,000,000, going to be further away from actual mean 
        - if sample is 500,000 out of 5million, will be closer to actual mean 
    
Central Limit Theorum 

    - with a large enough sample size the sampling distribution of the mean will be normally distributed
    - Applies to: 
        - Sample means 
        - Sample proportions
        - Difference in sample means (xbar1 - xbar2)
        - Difference in sample proportions
    - Pull samples and plot on histograms until it's bell shaped 
    - No matter how skewed a population is, it can still be distributed evenly with proper sampling

Recap:

Sampling Distributions

    Sampling Distributions are the distribution of a statistic (any statistic).
    There are two fundamental mathematical theorems that are related to sampling distributions: The Law of Large Numbers and The Central Limit Theorem.
    The Law of Large Numbers states that the sample mean will get closer to the population mean as the sample size increases. In general, if our statistic is a "good" estimate of a parameter, it will approach our parameter with larger sample sizes.
    The Central Limit Theorem states that our sample mean will follow a normal distribution with large enough sample sizes, but it turns out this is true for more than just the sample mean.

Bootstrapping
    Bootstrapping is a technique where we sample from a group with replacement.
    We can use bootstrapping to simulate the creation of sampling distribution, which you did many times in this lesson.
    By bootstrapping and then calculating repeated values of our statistics, we can understand the sampling distribution of our statistics.

Confidence Intervals

    - use bootstrapping and sampling distributions to build confidence intervals for our parameters of interest
    - np.sample() 
    - np.percentile(df, 2.5), np.percentile(df,97.5) ------ gives confidence interval of 5%, take 2.5 off bottom and top 


Practical vs Statistical Significance

    - Statistical Significance
        - Using confidence intervals and hypothesis testing

    - Practical
        - takes into consideration other factors of your situation
        - Constraints like space, time, or money are important

    - The confidence interval width is the difference between your upper and lower bounds of your confidence interval
    - margin of error is half the confidence interval width and the value that you add and subtract from your sample estimate to achieve your confidence interval final results.



Hypothesis Testing 

    -  collect data to justify which hypothesis is likely to be true
    - allow for the use of sample data to draw conclusions about an entire population
    - always performing hypothesis tests on population parameters, never on statistics
    - Statistics are values that you already have from the data, so it does not make sense to perform hypothesis tests on these values

    - Setting up Hypothesis Tests 

        - Null Hypothesis
            - the value we believe to be true before collecting any data 
            - H0 
        
        - Alternative Hypothesis 
            - What you want to prove to be true 
            - H1

    - Hypothesis Errors 

        - Type 1 Errors 
            - Claiming an innocent person as guilty 
            - False Positives
            - Alternative is chosen, but the null is actually true 
            - Worst of the two erros 
            - set up your null and alternative hypotheses so that the worse of your errors is the type I error.
            - denoted by the symbol α (alpha)
        
        - Type 2 Error
            - False Negatives
            - Setting a guilty person free 
            - Null is chosen but alternative is true 
            - denoted by the symbol β or beta 


    - P Values 
        - the probability of observing your statistic (or one more extreme in favor of the alternative) if the null hypothesis is true
        - Calculating 

            - Simulate the values of your statistic that are possible from the null
            - Calculate the value of the statistic you actually obtained in your data
            - Compare your statistic to the values from the null
            - Calculate the proportion of null values that are considered extreme based on your alternative

        - Closer to 1, accept null hypothesis 
        - Closer to 0, reject null hypothesis



    - Power analysis 

        - Used to estimate how long we need to run an experiment to see a specified impact in metrics


A/B Testing 

    - Version of hypothesis testing 
    - Old group / new group for website design 
    - Change Aversion 
        - Users may not respond well to a change and give more use to an older version 
        - Be cautious 
    - Novelty effect 
        - Users may like it just because its new 
    - Both noverlty effect and change aversion present biases 
    - Control Groups 
        - Using the original product or  feature 
    - Experiment Group 
        - Using the new version or product 
    - Click Through Rate 
        - Number of clicks divided by number of users that visit 
        - Use unique visitors who clicked at least once / unqique viewers
