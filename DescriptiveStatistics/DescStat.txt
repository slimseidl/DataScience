Descriptive Statistics Overview

Quantitative vs. Categorical Data Types 

    Quantitative 
        - Numerical values 
        - Height, age, numbers ,income, etc

        Continuous Data 
            - Can be split into smaller and smaller units all the time 
            - Age, can be broken into years, months, wekks, days ,etc 
            - Can be decimal values 
            - Height, age, income 
        Discrete Data 
            - Only takes on a number of values 
            - Counts 

    Categorical 
        - Groups / sets of items 
        - Gender ,grades, zip codes, etc.. 

        Ordinal Data 
            - Categorical values that are ranked / follow a natural order 
            - positive interaaction ratings 
            - Letter grade (A-F), Survey Ratings 
        Nominal 
            - Has no natural order 
            - i.e. dog breeds, fruits, etc.. 

Measures of Center 

    - Mean, median, mode 
    - median is in a sorted list 
    - Median for even values 
        - Take average of two middle numbers 
    - Mode = Frequency

Measures of Spread 

    Histograms 

        - Visual for spread, most common 
        - Bin data 
        - Create bar chart from bins to view spread of data 
            - center, shape, spread, outleirs 
    
    Five Number Summary 

        - Calc the Ranges 
            - Min 
            - Q1, Q2, Q3 
            - Max 
        - Order Values 
        - Median = Q2 
        - Q1 and Q3 = Medians on either side of whole median 
            - includes the min and max 
        - For even number of values, median is mean of two center values 
            - Q1 and Q3 = split dataset between two medians 
                - Q1 = median of lower half 
        - Range = Max - Min 
        - Interquartile Range = Q3 - Q1 
            - IQR 
    
        - Box Plots 
            - compare spread of datasets 
            - Use Spread 
            - Range is full length of plot 
            - IQR is box ends 
                - Q1 = Box Start 
                - Q3 = Box End
                - Q2 = Mid 
    
    Standard Deviation and Variance

        - Variance = the average squared difference of each observation from the mean
            - 1/n * Σ(xi - mean)^2 
            - Take all values minus the mean and square them 
            - Take the average of all squares 
            - used to compare the spread of two different groups
                - A set of data with higher variance is more spread out than a dataset with lower variance
                - Look for outliers first 
        - Std Dev = Squuare root of variance 
            - On average how far each observation is from the mean 

        - When data are related to money or the economy, higher variance (or standard deviation) is associated with higher risk
        - The standard deviation is used more often in practice than the variance because it shares the units of the original dataset.

Outliers and Shape 

    Shape 

        - Histograms show shape 
            - Shorter on left = left skew 
            - Shorter on right = right skew 
            - symmetric = normal / bell curve 
                - mean = median = mode 
        - Mode = tallest bar in Histograms

        - Bell Shaped Data 
            - Heights, Weight, Scores
            - mean = median = mode
        - Left Skew 
            - 	GPA, Age of Death, Price
            - Mean < median 
        - Right Skew 
            - Distribution of Wealth, Athletic Abilities
            - Mean > median 

    Outliers 

        - outliers are points that fall very far from the rest of our data points
        - quick plot shows a lot 
        - Box plots show outlier points 
        - Common Techniques for working with outliers 

            - Noting they exist and the impact on summary statistics.
            - If typo - remove or fix
            - Understanding why they exist, and the impact on questions we are trying to answer about our data.
            - Reporting the 5 number summary values is often a better indication than measures like the mean and standard deviation when we have outliers.
            - Be careful in reporting. Know how to ask the right questions.

            - Plot your data to identify if you have outliers.
            - Handle outliers accordingly via the previous methods.
            - If no outliers and your data follow a normal distribution - use the mean and standard deviation to describe your dataset, and report that the data are normally distributed.
            - If you have skewed data or outliers, use the five-number summary to summarize your data and report the outliers.
